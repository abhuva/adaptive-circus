# Research notes

This selective reading list records what each source contributes to the framework and where the inference ends. Page references to Quat and Botteri refer to the editions Marc supplied.

| Source and kind | Contribution and limit |
|---|---|
| Craig Quat, *Functional Juggling*, 2021, pp. 17–22, 41–43, 64–77. Practitioner book. | The separation of expression from experience, notation and responsive facilitation yield specific ways to vary an activity. The book’s claim that a facilitator can infer flow from synchronized movement would require independent evidence about the participant’s experience. |
| Lapo Botteri, *Study of the effects of Juggling sessions with the juggleboard on subjects with intellectual disability*, 2019, pp. 10–23. Local pilot. | Six participants received twelve individually adapted sessions. Six comparison participants continued usual activities. Assignment depended partly on attendance. The tablet task offers a more distinct test of transfer than the board pattern-copying task. Individual facilitation and the board were delivered together. The [original-table review](research/review.md#a-finding-recovered-from-botteris-figures) gives the group means and explains the limits of the within-group tests. |
| Wood, Bruner & Ross, 1976. Study of tutoring. [The role of tutoring in problem solving](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.1976.tb00381.x) | Reducing degrees of freedom is an explicit scaffolding function. Quat’s objection to drills that postpone the experience is compatible with scaffolding itself. |
| Newell, 1986. Theoretical chapter. [Constraints on the development of coordination](https://grants.hhp.uh.edu/clayne/HistoryofMC/Newell1986.pdf) | Person, task and environment jointly constrain movement. This supplies a way to generate hypotheses about difficult situations. It does not identify the limiting demand for an individual. |
| Law et al., 2011. Cluster randomized trial in young children with cerebral palsy. [Focus on function](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21569012/) | Context-focused and child-focused approaches had broadly comparable functional outcomes in the studied setting. Applying that principle in circus is an extrapolation. |
| Geller, Moringen & Friedman, 2023. Cascade practice experiment. [Learning juggling in parts or as a complete skill](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1284053) | The whole-skill group improved faster initially. The parts group caught up by the end of training. These short-term outcomes support keeping both approaches available. |
| Pinder et al., 2011. Conceptual paper. [Representative learning design](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21451175/) | An adaptation can preserve or remove information on which the participant acts. A live partner’s hesitation and a recording with the same timing offer different opportunities to influence the exchange. That example is our application. |
| Ryan & Deci, 2020. Theoretical overview. [Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/2020_RyanDeci_IntrinsicandExtrinsic.pdf) | Autonomy, competence and relatedness offer questions about experience. Choosing assistance can be an autonomous act. Visible independence or persistence alone tells us little about ownership. |
| [Participation in Community Leisure Programs](https://doi.org/10.1080/01490400.2017.1408509), 2018. Interviews with children and parents around a community circus program. | Fun, friendships, physical activity, coaching style and manageable challenge mattered to participants. Their accounts identify worthwhile outcomes without establishing that a specific technique produced them. |

The available field cases, pilot and adjacent research motivate the proposed design questions. Whether this particular framework helps different educators make better decisions is itself an open question.
