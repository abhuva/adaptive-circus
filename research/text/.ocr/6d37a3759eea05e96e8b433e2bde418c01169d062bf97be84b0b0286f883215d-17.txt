PRIMARY COMPOSITION - NOTES
Forward, Open-State
Here a new gesturing model must be instructed in order to setup initial conditions
of activity. This is easily done by rolling the first ball and gesturing to the
location you wish the participant to roll. Some participants can need several
repetitions of the gesturing instruction to get familiar. lt is always important to
take your time in developing awarenesses, particularly With this transition as
participants will be approaching peak level experiences and we wish to maintain
that positive influence.
This notation is similar to the one before, but introduces the two outer lanes and
requires the awareness to alternate locations of incoming information. Also, by
introducing the outer lanes it creates the space needed for suggesting the
following transition.
This notation is similar to the Fixed-State sequence (42)(35) except that it is
Asynchronous and Open-State. A good way to help encourage the alternating cross-over
motion of the [I —2] and [I —3] is by applying packeting technique. It seems illogical to
apply Synchronous like timing when there is only one lane to return them; however, the
influence of tempo and conditioned response impulse causes this not to be a complicated
problem for participants to solve. Simply, they align the balls in a row and send them
back simultaneously as they felt to arrive. From this state, separating the period of
packeting will cause participants to transition to intended Asynchronous Tempo.
This last sequence represents a three-way bi-lateral processing state in which the brain
must process the bi-lateral reflection of body, objects, and spatial symmetry intersecting.
This sequence is informally known as "Mills Mess" in toss juggling and represents a
series of movements that maximise bi-lateral processing communication.
