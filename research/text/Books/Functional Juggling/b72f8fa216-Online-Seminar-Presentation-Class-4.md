Primary Composition ➡ The primary composition is a radically eﬀective means

for developing a person’s sequencing capacity, well beyond what is needed to manage the expression of a 3-ball tossing cascade.

➡ It takes the Theory of Process Formations and applies its transformations to the systematic capacity development of each participant’s personal experience.

➡ It is intended (not required) to be facilitated none

verbally and with as little interruption to engagement as possible.

➡ Emphasis of social, emotional, and sensory integrated learning experience is critical for consistent delivery of beneficial outcomes.

## PDF page 7

Transition Beats ➡ Not all patterns can be sequenced without interruption or pause to their engagement. In these situations we use what is called a transition beat.

➡ This is indicated by a small letter t placed in front of notation, within the composition. Transitions are intended to be performed only once as a means of arriving from one pattern to another.

➡ This appears in the composition when moving in and out of patterns four and three.

## PDF page 11

Balanced Tempo ➡ Whenever facilitating Functional Juggling, it is very important that we learn to imitate the natural frequencies of our participant’s behaviours, first.

➡ In doing so, we create a relationship of Balanced Tempo with them, and allow each person to determine, or set, whatever level of challenge they are most comfortable with from the start.

➡ We exercise this tempo by releasing and receiving our objects at the same time, and with the same amount of force, as our participant.

Video Link: https://youtu.be/LhbN8sYRZis

## PDF page 12

Hand Positioning ➡ Refers to the manner in which facilitators interact with their objects. The best strategy for this is to relax our hands, as much as possible, with the fingertips extended and resting on top, and the thumbs placed behind.

➡ Important to contact objects while they remain in motion, otherwise communication channel will not be open.

➡ We want to be responsive to

frequencies of force in the same way that we are with sound. (listen through hands)

Video Link: https://youtu.be/KBfbZ8LYFRM

## PDF page 13

Tactile Management ➡ Increasing the amount of Force applied to an object will decrease the amount of Time it takes to travel a certain Distance; however, this direct style of communication is not always the best way to influence another person’s behaviour.

➡ Amplifying force introduces larger amounts of energy

into an activity, which can lead to over stimulation or even unmanageable levels of anxiety in some people.

➡ Similar to the way that a synchronised crowed of

clappers will influence the frequency of each other’s behaviour, and begin to clap more rapidly, over time.

## PDF page 14

Tactile Management ➡ To avoid these outcomes we

apply a sensory management strategy that focuses on developing a persons sense of time, more so than their sense of force.

➡ This is achieved by either shorten or lengthening the distance of interactions in between the facilitator and their participant.

➡ The goal is to increase the timing of events, while simultaneously communicating a soften of force to avoid overstimulation and to promote responsiveness to tactile inputs. Video Link: https://youtu.be/YIsEEPIE9z0

## PDF page 26

Flow State Stimulation ➡ As discussed in the previous presentation, our

experience of juggling can be defined by the state of mind which it produces: global activation.

➡ The behaviours of our brain and sensations of our body during a state of global activation are very similar, and associated to, the experiences of what we would call a flow state.

➡ Activation of flow state can be recognised as the instant a person’s frequency becomes completely harmonised with the interactions of their environment.

➡ This is detected in facilitation through the tactile sense

of objects, which helps us to paint a picture of the other person’s experiences.

## PDF page 27

Flow State Stimulation Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) ➡

In order to stimulate flow, it is necessary that we learn to appropriately manage the ZPD of each participant’s experience.

➡

ZPD can be thought of as the distance between what a person is able to do now, and what they could, potentially, be able to do next.
