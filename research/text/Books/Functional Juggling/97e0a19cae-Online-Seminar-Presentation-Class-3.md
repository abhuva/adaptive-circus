Previous Topics ➡ Functional Juggling?
• Determined by the intention of juggling and not by that of its design.
• Inclusive of all expressions regardless of accessibility or adaptation.
• Universal to the cause of its experience based outcomes of Global Activation and Flow State.

➡ Binary Forms
• Deconstructing the potential outcomes of our bilateral nervous system’s relationship to Space, Time, and Signal.

➡ Theory of Process Formations
• Formulating the deconstructed outcomes of our binary experience into an equation of eight possible outcomes.
• Each outcome represents a different sequencing relationship or frequency of information inside of the brain.
• This forms the basis of our behaviour outcome goals for developing and teaching juggling.

Previous Topics ➡ Static vs. Dynamic Stimulations
• Not all expressions of juggling are said to be equal and it is possible to express juggling without experiencing it authentically (which culturally we refer to as being dance).
• Removal of the need to process sensory information from motion through space, reduces the level of challenge and complexity of the activity.
• Although not considered a complete experience of juggling, Static interactions remain a valuable tool for compartmentalising learning developments and integrating functionality, as well.

Spatial Sequencing Maps ➡ Spatial sequencing maps are intended to

indicate where and when our interactions with space will occur, but do not determine anything about the manner in which they must be performed.

➡ By describing our activity in this way (as a spatial formula of its potential outcomes) we allow for the transformation of juggling’s expression, while still preserving the most transferable outcomes of its benefits (GLOBAL ACTIVATION).

➡ This freedom of transformation is what determines juggling to be one of the most accessible activities anyone could ever try to do in their life.
• It means that for whatever motion a person is

able to produce with their body, there is always going to be a way to correspond that motion to the harmonised sequencing of a relationship with space and time.

## PDF page 8

Applied Model ➡ Spatial sequencing maps can be made up of any

number of points; however, the smallest amount needed to be able to stimulate our entire spectrum of binary relationships is Five.

## PDF page 9

2-Points ➡ In order to stimulate these desired conditions, we must be able to interact with at least two-points of spatial information: one from each side of the body.

➡ With these points we can begin to engage the primary structures of the Theory of Process Formations.

## PDF page 10

3-Points ➡ In addition to two bilateral points of interaction, another centralised point of information is needed to be able to mark the midline separation between the two sides of our binary experiences.

➡ Combinations of these initial three points are enough to sequence most interactions with juggling; however, they do not provide enough opportunity, on their own, to formulate the entire spectrum of our possible relationships with space and time.

## PDF page 11

5-Points ➡ To achieve this condition, an additional two points of interaction must be added: one to either side of the body.

➡ This creates a total of five sequencing points, with one positioned directly in the centre, and two more positioned on either side of the midline.

➡ Most commonly, for facilitation, these points are arranged in a horizontal plane, in order to make their interaction more accessible. However, it is equally imaginable to arrange them in any order of height (y), depth (z), or width (x).

## PDF page 15

Propswap Notation ➡ Propswap is an ordered system of notation that allows us to describe the diﬀerent sequencing activities of Functional Juggling.

➡ It derives its grammatical influence from the classical model of siteswap notation, which is the mathematical language of juggling.

➡ In general, siteswap seeks to calculate the order of its events in time, whereas propswap acts to identify the location of events in space.

➡ Intended to create another universal language of

juggling that can be shared and described, without any additional need for its translation.

Reading and Writing Empty Beats ➡ There is one other integer that applies to both systems of arrangement and that is the value of zero.

➡ Zeros are used to indicate the skipping of one beat in time, or in other words, whenever one side of the i body interacts more than once before changing sides.

➡ This form of notation is more advanced and not something that is often used in general facilitations as it tends to require verbal communication to be able to explain.

Start Position ➡ For horizontal arrangements, each integer represents one sequencing point for either side of the body, so we are not able to determine its start position simply by the order of its notation alone.

➡ We resolve this issue by applying the settings of asynchronous time, and lateral initiation, from the start.

Asynchronous Bilateral ➡ Asynchronous bilateral notations describe sequences in which every beat alternates from side- to-side, and each beat crosses or exchanges its spatial information, bilaterally, from one side to the other.

➡ This type of notation is indicated by the use of an x symbol followed by a period, placed prior to the start of its notation.

➡ In general the x symbol is used to instruct the changing of our relationship with space, from lateral to bilateral or vice versa.

Synchronous Bilateral ➡ Synchronous bilateral notations describe sequences in which both sides of the body engage simultaneously, while also exchanging and sharing spatial information about their experiences between sides.

➡ Synchronous bilateral notation is written with the same parenthetical enclosures as its synchronous lateral counterpart, only with x indicators placed within parenthesis i to indicate the crossing or uncrossing of arms.

➡ An x at the beginning of a notation indicates that its

interaction should be performed bilaterally; however, an additional x positioned at the end of the same notation, would require its interaction to change or revert back to its previous condition.
