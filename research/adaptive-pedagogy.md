# What travels beyond circus?

**Working scope. Adaptive pedagogy designs a learning situation so a person can engage with a challenge they have reason to take on.** The useful unit is a person or group attempting something under specified conditions. Circus supplies detailed examples because its timing, spatial relations and division of work are often visible. A broader claim becomes credible one new setting at a time.

## Keep three decisions. Make two of them more precise.

1. **Choose the purpose, with the people affected.** Whose purpose is it, and who gets to revise it? *Creability* used workshops to change and sometimes discard activities proposed by designers. The Quebec study shows how an institution’s idea of progress can differ from what participants value. A person can choose a supported role as their artistic contribution. For a research record, distinguish a purpose someone expressed, one negotiated with them, and one an observer inferred. [Creability, pp. 135–141](text/Books/456d402cf7-Creability-Praxishandbuch-DE1.md#pdf-page-135) · [Spiegel & Parent, pp. 1–2](text/Research%20%2B%20Studies/Social%20Circus/c61bcd42a1-Re-approaching-community-development-through-the.md#pdf-page-1)
2. **Preserve an opportunity to practise the intended demand.** Describe it as something the learner can *notice and do*. Then identify the demand that may obstruct it. Add one detail when the adaptation uses a signal: **what information does this cue carry, and who produces it?** A cue can reveal what a partner is doing, give an answer away, or become a second rhythm the learner must track. Look for more than one plausible solution before inferring what capacity someone exercised. [Audio-tactile cue experiment, pp. 1–2](text/Research%20%2B%20Studies/6aeebc9f3f-Behavioral-Impact-of-Unisensory-and-Multisensory.md#pdf-page-1) · [Botteri’s generator, pp. 131–132](text/Books/ee202785e1-Motor-Capacities-Handbook-in-the-Educational-Cir.md#pdf-page-131)
3. **Describe who can change the course of the activity.** Who can initiate, vary, hold, decline or repair? Can another person perceive the change and respond to it? The performer’s control includes how other people take up a contribution. A right to stop that a partner cannot perceive is an incomplete design. Papadato’s proposal to offer two images as a way to choose equipment is documented as a proposed revision. It illustrates the distinction between designing a choice and observing someone exercise it. [Papadato, pp. 63–64](text/Research%20%2B%20Studies/Functional%20Juggling/9fab11f754-Elaborato-da-stampare.md#pdf-page-63) · [Creability, p. 31](text/Books/456d402cf7-Creability-Praxishandbuch-DE1.md#pdf-page-31)

Observation remains on either side of these decisions. Record the event before interpreting it. Afterwards match the evidence to the purpose: access now, a capacity demonstrated again later, use in another task, or what participation meant to someone. Carry the observer’s identity or role with the observation. For instance, an educator’s report that a child stayed in the room is different evidence from the child asking to come back.

## A worked example already present in the archive

In *Gemeinsam starten und gemeinsam stoppen*, an inclusive movement group tries to start and stop together. Players can move on the spot or travel. A clap or instrument can make a transition audible. A warm-up option lets the facilitator start and stop music. These are concrete variants offered by the authors. The following comparisons are our analysis. [Creability, PDF pp. 76–77](text/Books/456d402cf7-Creability-Praxishandbuch-DE1.md#pdf-page-76)

| Purpose | What the adaptation changes | What the learner still does |
|---|---|---|
| Join the group while staying in one place | Remove travel through the room. Keep the shared timing. | Choose movement and respond to or influence the group transition. |
| Practise responding to a clear stop | Facilitator cuts the music, making the event obvious. | Detect the external change and stop. |
| Practise noticing and shaping a collective decision | Let players initiate a stop. Make their cues perceptible to partners by a mutually chosen sound or gesture. | Propose a change, notice another person’s proposal, and decide whether to follow it. |

All three groups might stop together. Synchrony alone cannot identify which capacity was exercised. A more revealing observation is what happens when a player proposes a stop while the facilitator continues moving. Does anyone follow? Ask participants how they noticed the proposal and whether they wanted to lead. This is a proposed contrast, not a reported result of the workshop.

## A further transfer test in an ordinary classroom

**Hypothetical case.** A learner can explain fractions aloud but has trouble recording them in writing. If the purpose is comparing fractions, let a partner place the symbols at the learner’s direction. Leave the learner in charge of choosing the comparison, explaining it, and correcting a misplaced symbol. If the partner silently fixes every comparison, the page may be immaculate while the opportunity to compare has disappeared. If the purpose is learning to form the written symbols, the same division of labour removes the very action to be practised.

Check access on the first occasion. For learning, offer different fractions later with the same access to a scribe. Removing the scribe would introduce handwriting as a competing explanation again. This case illustrates a design consequence. Evidence for improved mathematics learning would require observations in mathematics.

## Implication for the research workflow

For article extraction, keep **purpose owner, intended demand, altered condition, cue source, participant-controlled action, observer and observed outcome** available as separate fields. Leave a field unknown if the source omits it. Compare cases where adding the same kind of support helped, interfered, or made no measurable difference. A useful first search is *who emits the cue when an activity becomes easier?* Search both the activity descriptions and the evaluations. A manual can supply a plausible paired contrast even where no outcome was measured.

The collection supports cross-domain design in the performing arts, with adjacent evidence from education and movement science. The small-group cases and task experiments offer mechanisms to test. The framework’s value for mathematics, literacy, or everyday learning remains a research question.
