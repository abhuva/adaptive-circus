# Open Directory · research overview

**Start with Creability, Botteri’s Motor Capacities’ Handbook, and the audio-tactile juggling experiment.** Together they answer three different questions: who can help design an adaptation, how to generate alternatives, and how to discover that a plausible adaptation made the task harder. The strongest bridge beyond circus is already in this collection: disabled and non-disabled artists and facilitators revising activities in music, dance and theatre.

This is a first-pass thematic reading of 53 PDFs (3,678 physical pages) and one flyer. Long manuals and theses were reviewed through contents, relevant chapters, methods and results; it is not a page-by-page critical appraisal. The folder includes overlapping editions, practitioner accounts, training slides, primary studies, theses and secondary collections. Use the [annotated catalogue](catalogue.md) for an entry on every file, and [the framework implications](adaptive-pedagogy.md) for the design proposal.

### A finding recovered from Botteri’s figures

The original PDF makes it possible to check the tables that were missing from the earlier pasted text. On the tablet task the training group mean increased from **13.00 to 16.28 targets in 15 seconds**, while the comparison mean went from **12.94 to 11.06**. For board sequencing, where lower was better, the reported means changed from **0.799 to 0.393** and **0.395 to 0.334** respectively. The groups started at quite different levels on this second measure. Each group had six participants; allocation depended partly on attendance. The pages show separate within-group paired tests, with a directional hypothesis for the training group. They do not report a direct test of the difference in improvement between groups. The tablet result makes transfer worth investigating. It cannot tell us whether the board, the facilitator’s adjustments, the additional practice, or their combination caused it. ### What to keep out of an evidence count

The two Botteri pilot files have identical hashes. The two English motor-handbook files yield identical embedded page text. Quat’s translated book, presentations and handouts belong to one practice tradition. The scientific collection points back to studies already present. NICA’s materials document what educators propose or offer. These are all worth keeping for retrieval; treating each file as an independent positive finding would inflate the evidence.
